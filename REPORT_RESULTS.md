## Threats to validity

1. Single camera, single day. Every 'measured on Dhaka' number comes from one fixed camera (clip 20261025_092449_side1_1.mp4 or the footage named in its source line); nothing here is shown to hold at another site, hour or weather.
2. Synthetic is not Dhaka. Rule decisions from tools/bench_accuracy.py, the impact check against simulated delay and the signal results come from simulators the team wrote; they test the code against the team's own model of traffic, so they can confirm a mechanism works but not that it matches the street.
3. Tiny counts. The simulated rule benchmark has 2-6 true events per rule; a single miss moves recall by 17-50 points. No confidence intervals are given where n is this small.
4. Proxy tracking is not accuracy. Without ground-truth tracks, ByteTrack and BoT-SORT can be compared only on cost, track counts and lengths and mutual agreement. Fewer or longer tracks may reflect merged identities rather than better tracking.
5. Timings were taken on a shared 6-core/12-thread laptop CPU (i5-11400H, no GPU) and may have been taken while other jobs ran; the load average is recorded beside each timing. Treat them as indicative to within a factor, not as benchmarks.
6. External numbers (CityFlowV2) are US traffic, copied from the notebook's printed output in research/cityflow/README.md, not re-run here, and do not predict Dhaka performance; the within-camera Dhaka check cannot see viewpoint change.
7. Hand labels are the ground truth for everything marked 'needs': their quality (one labeller, boundary ambiguity of 'when a violation starts') bounds every number that will eventually be reported. docs/EVALUATION.md asks for two raters on the subjective items.
8. Training/test separation: none of the detector's training split is recorded in the repository, so held-out status of any labelled Dhaka set must be established by the team before X1 is reported.
